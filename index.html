One Post

I've always thought that the charm of meeting someone in a spontaneous and open-ended environment can be carried through to the online world. Talking to a stranger, for 5 minutes, in a book store, without even learning each other's names, is a wonderful feature of living around many other people. But online these kinds of interactions are constrained to only a few spaces. In most of social media, so much about us is readily available and on the surface. The features and optimisation which platforms provide help us connect with the right people, sure, but they also sometimes dim the magic of meeting someone new.

So, One Post simply takes all of that away and makes it utterly straightforward to start up a conversation with a stranger, in the purity of just language, text. There are no profiles, followers, or likes. And beyond gender, age, and location, there is no way to filter through posts except to read them.

I've met some people in similar ways, online, who've become important in my life. And I hope to meet more people here. I'm optimistic about the prospect of interacting with strangers in such an unrestrained environment. On the other hand, the unrestrained nature of this app means that judgement and discretion are extremely important. I hope One Post can be a place where you can just start talking to anyone, and that's exciting. But, at best, it can only be this first step of forging a new social connection. Therefore the same safety instincts should be kept in mind as with interacting with any strangers, anywhere, online or otherwise.
